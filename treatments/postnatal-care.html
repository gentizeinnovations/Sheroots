Signs you may notice

  • Back or pelvic pain
  • A core that feels weak
  • Leaking urine
  • Discomfort around a caesarean scar or perineum

How physiotherapy helps

  • A check of your core and pelvic floor
  • A graded return to exercise
  • Advice on lifting and feeding positions
  • Guidance on scar care

Common reasons people come to us

  • Stretching of the core and pelvic floor during pregnancy
  • Vaginal birth, tears or an episiotomy
  • Caesarean section recovery
  • Feeding, lifting and carrying positions that strain the back and neck
  • Lack of sleep and time for yourself

What Postnatal Care sessions involve

We check your core, pelvic floor and any scar, and talk about your birth and how you are feeling now. Treatment includes gentle core and pelvic floor rehabilitation, safe positions for feeding and lifting, scar care where relevant, and a step-by-step return to exercise. You are welcome to bring your baby.

How long recovery can take

Recovery after birth takes months, not weeks, and every birth is different. Gentle exercise can usually start soon after birth, with higher-impact activity such as running introduced gradually once your body is ready.

What to expect at your first visit

  1. We listenA relaxed conversation about your history, your goals and what worries you.
  2. We assessA careful assessment to find the cause, with every step explained first.
  3. We plan togetherHands-on care, guided exercise and a home plan made for you.

Simple things that help at home

  • Start gentle pelvic floor and breathing exercises when comfortable
  • Support your back with pillows while feeding
  • Bend your knees and keep your baby close when lifting
  • Rest when you can, and ask for help

See a doctor first if you have

  • Heavy bleeding, large clots or foul-smelling discharge
  • Fever, or a red, hot or weeping wound
  • Calf pain or swelling, chest pain or sudden breathlessness
  • Feeling very low, anxious or unable to cope

Postnatal Care: common questions

When can I start postnatal physiotherapy?

Gentle advice and exercises can begin early. A full check is often helpful around six weeks after birth, or once your doctor is happy with your recovery.

Is it safe after a caesarean?

Yes, with a plan adjusted to your scar and recovery. We follow any advice from your doctor.

When can I return to running or the gym?

We check your strength and pelvic floor first, then guide a gradual return. Many women need a few months before high-impact exercise.

Is this right for me?

Every plan starts with an assessment. We will tell you honestly if physiotherapy suits you, or if you should see another health professional.

What should I bring?

Comfortable clothes you can move in, and any scan reports, referral letters or a list of your medicines.
