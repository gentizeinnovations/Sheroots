Shoulder pain can follow overuse or injury, or come with stiffness such as frozen shoulder, where movement becomes painful and limited.

Signs you may notice

  • Pain when lifting your arm or reaching behind you
  • An ache that is worse at night
  • Stiffness and limited movement
  • Weakness when using the arm

How physiotherapy helps

  • Gentle mobilisation to improve movement
  • Graded exercises to rebuild strength
  • Techniques to ease pain
  • Guidance on everyday activities and pacing

What can cause it?

  • Repeated reaching, lifting or overhead work
  • Irritated or weak rotator cuff tendons
  • Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis), which is more common in people with diabetes and in midlife
  • Falls or injuries such as a strain or dislocation
  • Rounded posture that changes how the shoulder blade moves

What Shoulder Pain & Frozen Shoulder Physiotherapy sessions involve

We first check how your shoulder and shoulder blade move, which movements hurt, and whether the neck is involved. Treatment depends on the cause: for tendon problems the focus is gradual strengthening, while for frozen shoulder we use gentle mobilisation and stretches within your comfort. You will leave each session knowing exactly what to practise at home.

How long recovery can take

Shoulder strains and tendon problems often improve over several weeks to a few months of steady exercise. Frozen shoulder is different: it tends to move through painful, stiff and recovery stages and can take many months to settle. Physiotherapy aims to keep you as comfortable and mobile as possible along the way.

What to expect at your first visit

  1. We listenA relaxed conversation about your history, your goals and what worries you.
  2. We assessA careful assessment to find the cause, with every step explained first.
  3. We plan togetherHands-on care, guided exercise and a home plan made for you.

Simple things that help at home

  • Keep the arm moving gently within a comfortable range
  • Avoid sleeping on the painful side; hug a pillow for support
  • Use a warm pack before your exercises if it helps stiffness
  • Break heavy or overhead tasks into shorter spells

See a doctor first if you have

  • Shoulder or arm pain with chest pain, breathlessness or sweating (call emergency services)
  • Being unable to lift the arm at all after a fall or injury
  • A shoulder that looks out of place
  • A hot, red, swollen joint or fever

Shoulder Pain & Frozen Shoulder: common questions

How do I know if I have a frozen shoulder?

Frozen shoulder usually causes pain and a steady loss of movement in every direction, especially turning the arm outwards. A physiotherapy assessment, and sometimes your doctor, can confirm it.

Should I rest my shoulder completely?

Complete rest usually makes a stiff shoulder stiffer. We will show you which movements are safe and how much to do.

Can physiotherapy help after shoulder surgery?

Yes. Rehabilitation after surgery follows your surgeon's protocol, and we guide you step by step through each stage.

Is this right for me?

Every plan starts with an assessment. We will tell you honestly if physiotherapy suits you, or if you should see another health professional.

What should I bring?

Comfortable clothes you can move in, and any scan reports, referral letters or a list of your medicines.
