Pregnancy changes your posture, joints and core. Physiotherapy supports your comfort and helps you prepare for birth and recovery.

Signs you may notice

  • Low back or pelvic girdle pain
  • Hip pain
  • Trouble sleeping or moving comfortably
  • Worry about exercising safely

How physiotherapy helps

  • Safe exercise guidance
  • Techniques to ease pain
  • Exercises to prepare for birth
  • Advice on posture and daily tasks

Common reasons people come to us

  • Hormonal changes that make ligaments more flexible
  • A growing bump that changes posture and balance
  • Extra load on the back, pelvis and hips
  • Reduced activity due to tiredness or worry
  • Previous back or pelvic pain

What Pregnancy (Antenatal) Care sessions involve

We ask about your pregnancy, your doctor's advice and what you would like to feel more confident about. Sessions can include safe exercise, techniques to ease back and pelvic pain, pelvic floor and breathing work, and preparation for birth and recovery. We always work within your doctor's guidance.

How long recovery can take

Support can start at any stage of pregnancy. Many women find pain becomes easier to manage within a few sessions, and keeping active through pregnancy helps with birth preparation and recovery.

What to expect at your first visit

  1. We listenA relaxed conversation about your history, your goals and what worries you.
  2. We assessA careful assessment to find the cause, with every step explained first.
  3. We plan togetherHands-on care, guided exercise and a home plan made for you.

Simple things that help at home

  • Stay active with walking, swimming or approved exercise
  • Sleep on your side with a pillow between your knees
  • Keep your knees together when getting in and out of bed or a car
  • Practise your pelvic floor and breathing exercises

See a doctor first if you have

  • Vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking
  • Severe headache, vision changes or sudden swelling of the face or hands
  • Reduced baby movements
  • Regular tightenings or pain before 37 weeks

Pregnancy (Antenatal) Care: common questions

Is it safe to exercise during pregnancy?

For most healthy pregnancies, regular moderate exercise is recommended. Always check with your doctor, and we will adapt exercises to you.

Can physiotherapy help pelvic girdle pain?

Yes. Advice, exercises and practical techniques often make pelvic girdle pain much easier to manage.

When should I start pregnancy physiotherapy?

Any time. Some women come early for exercise advice, others when pain starts or as birth approaches.

Is this right for me?

Every plan starts with an assessment. We will tell you honestly if physiotherapy suits you, or if you should see another health professional.

What should I bring?

Comfortable clothes you can move in, and any scan reports, referral letters or a list of your medicines.

This page is general information, not a diagnosis or medical advice. Results vary from person to person. If you have severe or sudden symptoms, please seek urgent medical care.
