Knee pain can come from an injury, overuse, or wear-and-tear changes in the joint (osteoarthritis).

Signs you may notice

  • Pain on stairs or when squatting
  • Stiffness after resting
  • Swelling around the knee
  • Clicking or a feeling of giving way

How physiotherapy helps

  • Strengthening for the hip and knee
  • Exercises to keep the joint moving
  • Guidance on activity and pacing
  • Walking and movement advice

What can cause it?

  • Age-related wear in the joint (osteoarthritis)
  • Weak thigh and hip muscles that leave the knee less supported
  • Twisting injuries to ligaments or the meniscus
  • Pain around the kneecap from overuse, stairs or squatting
  • Extra body weight, which increases load on the knee

What Knee Pain & Joint Degeneration Physiotherapy sessions involve

We assess how you walk, climb stairs and squat, and test the strength of the muscles around the hip and knee. Treatment focuses on strengthening exercises that support the joint, movement work for stiffness, and practical advice on walking, stairs and sitting on the floor, so you can keep doing the things that matter to you.

How long recovery can take

Knee pain from overuse often improves over several weeks of strengthening. For osteoarthritis, exercise is a long-term habit rather than a short course, and many people find their pain and walking improve as their muscles get stronger.

What to expect at your first visit

  1. We listenA relaxed conversation about your history, your goals and what worries you.
  2. We assessA careful assessment to find the cause, with every step explained first.
  3. We plan togetherHands-on care, guided exercise and a home plan made for you.

Simple things that help at home

  • Keep active with walking or cycling at a comfortable level
  • Do your strengthening exercises on most days
  • Use a handrail on stairs while the knee is painful
  • Avoid long periods kneeling or squatting during flare-ups

See a doctor first if you have

  • A knee that is hot, red and swollen, especially with fever
  • Being unable to put weight on the leg after an injury
  • A knee that locks and cannot be straightened
  • Calf pain and swelling, which can be a sign of a blood clot

Knee Pain & Joint Degeneration: common questions

Will exercise make my knee arthritis worse?

The right exercise is one of the most helpful treatments for knee osteoarthritis. We start at a level that suits you and build up gradually.

Can physiotherapy help me avoid knee surgery?

For many people, exercise and activity changes reduce pain enough that surgery is not needed. If surgery is the right choice, physiotherapy before and after helps recovery.

Can I sit cross-legged or on the floor?

It depends on your knee. We will look at what is comfortable for you and work on the movement if it matters in your daily life.

Is this right for me?

Every plan starts with an assessment. We will tell you honestly if physiotherapy suits you, or if you should see another health professional.

What should I bring?

Comfortable clothes you can move in, and any scan reports, referral letters or a list of your medicines.
