Neck pain is very common. It is often linked to long hours at a desk or on a phone, poor posture, or age-related changes in the neck joints (cervical spondylosis).

Signs you may notice

  • Stiffness or aching in the neck
  • Pain that spreads to the shoulders or arms
  • Headaches that start from the neck
  • Difficulty turning your head

How physiotherapy helps

  • Hands-on techniques to ease stiff joints and tight muscles
  • Targeted exercises for the neck and shoulder muscles
  • Advice on posture and workstation set-up
  • A home programme to keep you moving

What can cause it?

  • Long hours looking down at a phone, laptop or paperwork
  • A desk, chair or screen set at the wrong height
  • Sleeping with too many pillows or in an awkward position
  • Age-related wear in the neck joints and discs (cervical spondylosis)
  • Stress, which often shows up as tight neck and shoulder muscles

What Neck Pain & Cervical Spondylosis Physiotherapy sessions involve

Your first session looks at how your neck moves, which positions bring on the pain, and whether nerves to the arms are involved. Treatment usually combines gentle hands-on work for stiff joints and tight muscles with a few simple exercises to strengthen the deep neck and upper-back muscles. We also look at your work and phone habits, because small changes there often make the biggest difference.

How long recovery can take

Many people with a recent stiff or aching neck feel easier within a few weeks of regular exercise. Neck pain linked to long-standing cervical spondylosis usually needs a longer plan focused on staying flexible and strong, with flare-ups managed early. Everyone is different, and we review your progress at each visit.

What to expect at your first visit

  1. We listenA relaxed conversation about your history, your goals and what worries you.
  2. We assessA careful assessment to find the cause, with every step explained first.
  3. We plan togetherHands-on care, guided exercise and a home plan made for you.

Simple things that help at home

  • Raise your screen so the top of it is at eye level
  • Take a short movement break every 30 to 45 minutes
  • Use one supportive pillow that keeps your neck level
  • Do your home exercises little and often, not all at once

See a doctor first if you have

  • Neck pain after a fall, accident or blow to the head
  • Weakness, clumsiness or numbness in both arms or hands
  • Problems with balance or walking
  • Neck pain with fever, severe headache or a stiff neck you cannot bend forward

Neck Pain & Cervical Spondylosis: common questions

Is cervical spondylosis curable?

Cervical spondylosis describes age-related changes in the neck, which are very common. The changes themselves do not reverse, but many people have little or no pain with the right exercise, posture habits and activity.

Should I wear a neck collar?

For most everyday neck pain, staying gently active is better than wearing a collar for long periods. If a doctor has advised a collar after an injury, follow that advice and we will work around it.

Can neck problems cause headaches or arm pain?

Yes. Tight muscles and stiff joints in the neck can cause headaches, and an irritated nerve can send pain, tingling or numbness into the arm. Your assessment helps us find where it is coming from.

Is this right for me?

Every plan starts with an assessment. We will tell you honestly if physiotherapy suits you, or if you should see another health professional.

What should I bring?

Comfortable clothes you can move in, and any scan reports, referral letters or a list of your medicines.
