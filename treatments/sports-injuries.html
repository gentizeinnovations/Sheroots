Sprains, strains and overuse problems can happen in any sport or training. Rehab helps you recover and get back with confidence.

Signs you may notice

  • Sudden pain during activity
  • Swelling or bruising
  • Pain that comes back when you train
  • A drop in performance

How physiotherapy helps

  • Assessment of the injury and how you move
  • A structured rehabilitation plan
  • Strength and conditioning for return to activity
  • Advice to help reduce the chance of it happening again

What can cause it?

  • A sudden increase in training load or intensity
  • Poor warm-up or recovery
  • Muscle imbalances or reduced flexibility
  • Technique problems or unsuitable footwear and equipment
  • Returning too early after a previous injury

What Sports Injuries Physiotherapy sessions involve

We assess the injured area and look at how you move during the activities you do. Your plan moves in stages: settling pain, restoring movement, rebuilding strength and power, and finally sport-specific work, so you return with confidence. We also look at training habits that may have contributed.

How long recovery can take

Recovery time depends on the type and severity of the injury, from a couple of weeks for a minor strain to several months for more serious problems. We use clear milestones rather than guesswork to decide when you are ready to return.

What to expect at your first visit

  1. We listenA relaxed conversation about your history, your goals and what worries you.
  2. We assessA careful assessment to find the cause, with every step explained first.
  3. We plan togetherHands-on care, guided exercise and a home plan made for you.

Simple things that help at home

  • Stay active with exercise that does not aggravate the injury
  • Follow the stages of your plan rather than rushing back
  • Sleep well and eat well to support healing
  • Warm up properly before training

See a doctor first if you have

  • A 'pop' with immediate swelling and being unable to bear weight
  • A joint that looks deformed
  • Any head injury with confusion, vomiting or loss of consciousness
  • Numbness, coldness or colour change in a limb

Sports Injuries: common questions

When can I go back to my sport?

When you have met clear goals for strength, movement and confidence, not just when the pain stops. We test this together.

Can I keep training while injured?

Often you can keep training in a modified way. We help you find what is safe so you stay fit while recovering.

Do you see non-professional athletes?

Yes. Whether you walk, run, play cricket or go to the gym, the same principles apply.

Is this right for me?

Every plan starts with an assessment. We will tell you honestly if physiotherapy suits you, or if you should see another health professional.

What should I bring?

Comfortable clothes you can move in, and any scan reports, referral letters or a list of your medicines.

This page is general information, not a diagnosis or medical advice. Results vary from person to person. If you have severe or sudden symptoms, please seek urgent medical care.
