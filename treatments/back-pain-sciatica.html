Back pain is one of the most common reasons people see a physiotherapist. Sciatica is pain that travels from the lower back or buttock down the leg, often from an irritated nerve.

Signs you may notice

  • Aching or sharp pain in the lower back
  • Pain, tingling or numbness down the leg
  • Stiffness after sitting
  • Pain when bending or lifting

How physiotherapy helps

  • Hands-on treatment to ease pain and stiffness
  • Exercises to move and strengthen your back and core
  • Education on pacing and returning to activity
  • Advice on lifting, sitting and sleeping positions

What can cause it?

  • Sitting for long periods, especially with little movement
  • Lifting or bending awkwardly
  • Weak or deconditioned back, hip and core muscles
  • Disc changes that irritate a nerve and cause sciatica
  • Pregnancy, weight changes or stress

What Back Pain & Sciatica Physiotherapy sessions involve

Your assessment covers how your back moves, nerve tests for the legs, and the activities that ease or worsen your pain. Treatment combines hands-on techniques to reduce pain and stiffness with exercises to move and strengthen the back, hips and core. We also explain what is happening in plain words, because understanding your pain is an important part of recovery.

How long recovery can take

Many episodes of low back pain improve within a few weeks, and sciatica often settles over a few weeks to a few months. Staying gently active usually helps more than bed rest. If pain keeps coming back, we focus on building strength and confidence to reduce future flare-ups.

What to expect at your first visit

  1. We listenA relaxed conversation about your history, your goals and what worries you.
  2. We assessA careful assessment to find the cause, with every step explained first.
  3. We plan togetherHands-on care, guided exercise and a home plan made for you.

Simple things that help at home

  • Keep moving: short, frequent walks are often helpful
  • Change position regularly when sitting
  • Bend your knees and keep loads close when lifting
  • Use heat if it eases muscle spasm

See a doctor first if you have

  • Numbness around the groin, inner thighs or back passage
  • New difficulty passing urine, or loss of bladder or bowel control
  • Weakness in both legs or a foot that drags
  • Back pain with fever, unexplained weight loss, or after a significant fall

Back Pain & Sciatica: common questions

Should I rest in bed with back pain?

For most back pain, staying gently active is better than bed rest. We will show you safe ways to move and stay active.

Do I need an MRI scan?

Most back pain does not need a scan. Your doctor may advise one if there are warning signs or if symptoms do not improve as expected.

What is the difference between back pain and sciatica?

Back pain is felt mainly in the back. Sciatica is pain, tingling or numbness that travels down the leg, usually from an irritated nerve in the lower back.

Is this right for me?

Every plan starts with an assessment. We will tell you honestly if physiotherapy suits you, or if you should see another health professional.

What should I bring?

Comfortable clothes you can move in, and any scan reports, referral letters or a list of your medicines.
